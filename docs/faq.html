FAQ

Echoel Studio, in TestFlight now — and the earlier app (10.79), which is frozen

Echoel Studio, in TestFlight

What is Echoel Studio?
A music workstation for iPhone and iPad, in TestFlight and still being tested on devices: instruments, effects, a piano roll and a drum grid, an arrangement timeline, a session grid for live sets, audio and MIDI recording, mixing, automation and export. Your songs are files on your device in an open, documented format, and the app needs no account. The Roadmap lists everything this build holds.
Which instruments and effects does it have?
A 16-voice synth with six engines (Classic, Supersaw, FM, Sub, Morph and Mallet), a synthesized eight-piece drum kit with a step grid, and a sampler that plays an imported audio file across the keyboard — played from the on-screen keys or a MIDI keyboard. Every track and the master have an insert chain of up to eight effects. Eighteen built-in effects, among them EQ, compressor, delay, reverb, chorus, phaser, flanger, limiter, saturation, tape, pitch correction, a harmonizer and a granular effect (in TestFlight, not yet checked on a device). Installed stereo Audio Unit instruments and effects from other apps play on tracks too; that is not yet checked on a device either.
Can I record, and what does it export?
Record a MIDI performance onto an armed instrument track — each take becomes a new clip with its notes, pitch bend and controllers — or mono audio from the microphone or one interface input onto an armed audio track; takes stay on the device. Export the arrangement as a 24-bit, 48 kHz stereo WAV with its loudness (LUFS) and true peak reported, as stems (one WAV per track, before the master effects), or as a Standard MIDI File (.mid).
Does Echoel Studio read my body?
A Bluetooth heart-rate chest strap can be a modulation source: route heart rate, HRV or coherence to a fader, pan, send or synth parameter, with depth and inversion per route. With no strap, the control stays where you set it, and an export never reads the strap. Apple Health is not in Echoel Studio. What the app reads, and what it keeps, is on the Privacy page. Body values are for self-observation, not a medical diagnosis.
Can it stream, send OSC or drive lights?
Yes — each off until you switch it on, and none of it checked on a device yet. The Connections page sends OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net and sACN out to your local network; body values are sent only while you allow body sharing on that page. Go Live streams the Field’s picture and the master mix over RTMP, RTMPS or SRT to a service you choose; nothing goes out until you press Go Live. Video tracks bring a video from the photo library onto the timeline, and MPE out sends the live track’s notes to the MIDI outputs you pick, one channel per note.
Which devices does it run on?
iPhone and iPad with iOS 18 or later. Mac, Apple Watch, Apple TV and Vision Pro are planned.
What does it cost?
The app is free — no ads, no tracking, no account. A one-time purchase for Echoel’s own instrument plugins, for use in other music apps, is planned; there is no subscription, and nothing is on sale yet.
Who owns what I create?
You do. 100%. Everything you create with Echoel — songs, recordings, exports — belongs entirely to you. No royalty claims, no usage restrictions. Use commercially, remix, distribute — it’s yours. We claim no rights to user-generated content.
Is this a medical device?
No. Echoel is a creative tool, not a medical device. Heart rate, HRV and coherence are shown for creative use and self-observation only; they are not clinically validated measurements. Always consult healthcare professionals for medical advice. See our Health Disclaimer.
Is my data safe?
Your songs and takes stay on your device. No account, no analytics, no tracking or advertising SDKs, and no server of ours. Your device’s own Data Protection encrypts local storage. What leaves the device — only what you switch on — is listed in the Privacy Policy; see also the Security page.
Is Echoel Studio accessible?
It has VoiceOver labels on the primary controls and a dark, high-contrast interface. Reduce Motion is not read yet, and the accessibility check App Store 1.0 has to pass has not been run; the full list of gaps is on the Accessibility page. The app is in English.

The earlier app (10.79, frozen)

Every question below describes the earlier app, version 10.79.515, which played sound, visuals and light from the body. It is frozen and no longer in TestFlight; which of its parts return to Echoel Studio, and when, is on the Roadmap.

General (earlier app)

What was the earlier app?
The earlier Echoelmusic app was a bio-reactive generative music instrument that transformed your biometric signals (heart rate, HRV, breathing) into harmonic, melodic and rhythmic loops in real time, which you exported as .wav or as a MIDI file (.mid) for your DAW. It had GPU bio-reactive visuals, Art-Net/sACN lighting output and, in its last builds, a Workstation that arranged parts on tracks (note editor, .mid and audio import); video editing, audio recording and live broadcast were not part of it. It ran on iPhone.
Which features did it have?
The earlier app was a bio-reactive music instrument: bio-reactive synthesis (EchoelSynth), generative composition of harmonic, melodic and rhythmic loops in any key and BPM (EchoelGen), GPU/Metal bio-reactive visuals in a movable floating window, or full-screen on a connected display (EchoelVis, flash-safe), Art-Net and sACN lighting output (EchoelLux), export to .wav or .mid for Ableton, Logic and FL Studio, a Workstation that arranged parts on tracks with a note editor and .mid and audio import, MIDI 2.0-ready input from an external controller (EchoelMIDI), biometrics (EchoelBio — HRV, heart rate, breath via HealthKit, Bluetooth straps like Polar H10, and camera rPPG with a live control display), OSC and ADM-OSC sync (EchoelNet), and coherence / self-observation. DMX-512 interfaces, projection mapping, on-device AI, spatial audio and Ableton Link were on its roadmap. Video editing, multitrack audio recording and live streaming were not in that app. Three different histories, not one: the video editor was built and removed in July 2026 and video recording in September 2026; multitrack audio recording was removed in September 2026 with the audio input; live streaming was never built — the publisher is a compile-guarded scaffold and its library was never linked. The Architecture page lists its status at the freeze.
What made it "bio-reactive"?
Bio-reactive meant the software responded to your body in real time. EchoelBio captured your biometrics — HRV, heart rate, breath, and coherence — and fed them to the sound engine in real time. Your heart rate influenced tempo, filter brightness and vibrato; your coherence level was the strongest single driver — brightness, filter cutoff, harmonic content, level and noise; HRV shifted brightness and overtone spread; and your breathing shaped the amplitude envelope. Your body became the controller.
Did it need special hardware?
No — just an iPhone. The camera pulse worked with no extra hardware: a fingertip over the rear camera + flash, and the app read your heartbeat (photoplethysmography) in real time. For the lowest latency and HRV ground-truth you paired a standard Bluetooth heart-rate device (Polar, Wahoo, Garmin/CooSpo straps, or a watch in HR-broadcast mode). An Apple Watch worked via Apple Health (slightly higher latency). For MIDI, any class-compliant controller connected instantly.
Did it work with my Oura Ring or smartwatch?
Yes, via Apple Health — with your device's Apple Health sync on (Oura, Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Watch, etc.) the earlier app read heart rate and HRV from there. One honest caveat about latency: the Oura Ring (incl. Ring 4) exposes no real-time Bluetooth to third-party apps — it pairs only with the Oura app, and its data reached the app through Apple Health with a delay (not beat-to-beat). For live heart rate, the camera pulse or a standard Bluetooth heart-rate strap was the way.
What were the EchoelTools?
The earlier app's engines were organized into unified tools. Some shipped; the rest were the wider vision:
  • EchoelSynth — Bio-reactive synthesis: a DDSP harmonic-plus-noise voice, a polyphonic pad/lead and a dedicated sub-bass
  • EchoelGen — Generative composition: harmonic, melodic & rhythmic loops in any key and BPM
  • EchoelFX — Filter, saturation, tape, bitcrush, chorus/flanger/phaser/tremolo, delay, reverb, widener, compressor, limiter (modelled analog compressors Planned)
  • EchoelMIDI — MIDI 2.0-ready input (notes, pitch bend, Slide CC 74, Press/channel pressure); virtual MIDI 1.0 out behind a routing switch, and MPE output behind two more. MPE input zones and Audio→MIDI Planned
  • EchoelBio — HRV, heart rate, breath & LF/HF analysis (HealthKit, Bluetooth straps, camera rPPG)
  • EchoelNet — OSC & ADM-OSC immersive object output
  • EchoelVis — GPU/Metal bio-reactive visuals: four generative looks, flash-safe, floating window on the phone, or full-screen on a connected display — and playable: touch it and your fingers become in-key notes
  • EchoelLux — Art-Net + sACN lighting output over open standards (DMX-512 interfaces & smart home Planned)
  • EchoelMix Live — the Mixer stage: level, pan, mute and solo per track with undo, plus LUFS-aware mastering on export; multi-track audio recording needed an audio input, which that app did not have
  • EchoelVid Cut — video editing was built and removed in July 2026 and video recording in September 2026; neither was in that app at its freeze. RTMP was never built
  • EchoelStage Live — External-display output shipped (USB-C/HDMI or AirPlay); projection mapping Planned
  • EchoelAI Planned — On-device CoreML intelligence & AI stem separation
Every tool talked to every other tool in real time.

Instruments & Synthesis (earlier app)

What synthesis engines did it include?
All synthesis was unified inside EchoelSynth — one tool with three voices:
  • DDSP — Differentiable DSP (harmonic + noise model), the bio-reactive core
  • Poly — a polyphonic pad/lead with filter, envelope, unison and vibrato
  • Sub — a dedicated sub-bass voice you feel as much as hear
Your biometrics composed harmonic and melodic material in any key and BPM — pads, chords, leads and a sub-bass voice — mapped directly to sound parameters in real time. It had no drum engine (percussion was removed in July 2026). A sampler per track shipped in its last builds: a sample loaded onto a track played its parts. A take rendered to .wav, or exported as a MIDI file (.mid), for your DAW.
Could I play it with my hands?
The living picture was playable. Touching the bio-reactive visual — in the floating window on the phone or full-screen on a connected display, at every size — turned your fingers into notes, quantized to the take's key and scale, on the take's own sound, with water rings spreading under each touch. Every touch landed in key, so there was no wrong place to put a finger. The primary instrument was still your body; the surface was a second pair of hands on the same take. Any external MIDI controller you plugged in played the built-in performer voice — one monophonic voice, driven by notes, pitch bend, pressure and slide. In the app you shaped the sound in the patch editor and cut what your body played into loops; in the Workstation you could open a MIDI part in its note editor to add, move, lengthen or delete notes, or record a new part from your MIDI controller. It had no drum grid. Dedicated pad instruments (chord pad, melody XY pad, multi-octave keyboard, strum pad) were on its roadmap and never built. Echoel Studio has a drum step grid and on-screen keys.
Did it have orchestral instruments?
The earlier app was a synthesizer, not a sample library. EchoelSynth offered naturally-voiced DDSP timbre presets — violin, cello, clarinet, oboe, flute, trumpet and more: living synthesis models shaped by your body in real time, not multisampled orchestral sections. Full orchestral libraries with articulations were not a goal.

Effects & DSP (earlier app)

How many effects did it have?
All effects were unified inside EchoelFX — one bio-reactive chain, built natively with zero external dependencies: reverb, tape-style delay, chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo, saturation, bitcrush, stereo widener and filter, plus LoFi characters (Underwater, Telephone, Cassette, Vinyl, Dream, Megaphone) and a mastering stage (EQ → compressor → limiter with true-peak trim). Body signals could modulate FX parameters live. Every effect was a real DSP algorithm — nothing was a placeholder.
What analog-inspired processing did it include?
The saturation stage added analog-style drive and warmth, the tape-style delay brought wow-and-flutter echo character, and the LoFi characters (Cassette, Vinyl, Telephone and more) gave a take instant vintage texture. A dedicated suite of named analog emulations was on the roadmap and never built into it.

Composing & Music Creation (earlier app)

Did it compose music for me?
No — by design. The earlier app was a creativity tool, not an AI that generates finished pieces. Your biometric data guided the musical parameters — tempo from your heart rate, harmonic variation from your HRV, dynamic expression from your breathing — but you were always the creator.
What composing tools did it have?
EchoelGen composed from your body:
  • Generative loops — Harmonic, melodic and rhythmic loops composed in real time, in any key and BPM
  • Bio-reactive variation — The composition mutated with your bio-state: a slower, steadier pulse produced ordered, settled phrases; a faster, more variable one added variation
  • Coherence & self-observation — Lock to a BPM and create, or breathe and watch the music respond
  • Export — any take rendered to .wav or exported as a MIDI file (.mid), ready for Ableton, Logic or FL Studio

MIDI & Hardware (earlier app)

Where is the list of every OSC address, port and DMX channel the earlier app sent?
On the integrations page — the OSC bio feed (UDP 8000), ADM-OSC objects (UDP 4001), the virtual MIDI source, and the Art-Net (6454) / sACN (5568) channel map, with step-by-step spokes for Reaper, TouchDesigner and Resolume.
What MIDI features did it have?
All MIDI lived inside EchoelMIDI. MIDI 2.0-ready input (Universal MIDI Packets — parses both 1.0 and 2.0 traffic): notes and pitch bend reached the built-in performer voice — one monophonic voice. Slide (CC 74) opened that voice’s filter and Press (channel pressure) swelled it — both channel-wide, both reaching the one voice. Air-CC (CC 21–31) was parsed and carried on the control bus, but no voice consumed it. Output was a virtual “Echoelmusic” MIDI 1.0 source any DAW could record, created once you enabled a MIDI route in the routing panel. MPE output shipped: with the two switches in the routing panel on, notes went out over member channels 2–16, each carrying Glide (pitch bend), Slide (CC 74) and Press (channel pressure), with the zone announced. A second source, “Echoelmusic (MIDI 2.0)”, mirrored the same notes in the MIDI 2.0 protocol (16-bit velocity, 32-bit bend and controllers) behind its own routing switch (not yet verified on a device). MPE input zone handling (per-note channels, pressure), per-note MIDI 2.0 expression, inter-app tempo sync and audio-to-MIDI were on the roadmap, not in that app.
What about its recording and export?
The earlier app exported your take two ways: WAV (lossless) — auto-named with artist, date, key and BPM — and Standard MIDI File (.mid), which carried the melody with tempo, 4/4 and the key signature, anchored to whole bars so it landed on the grid in Ableton, Logic or FL Studio and looped seamlessly. The MIDI file was melody only; it had no drum track, because there were no drums in that instrument. Its Workstation imported a .mid file or an audio file onto a track. It had no audio recording — no microphone input — and no stem export or FLAC/ALAC; video editing was not part of it. Echoel Studio records audio and exports stems (see the first questions on this page).
Did it work as a plugin or a host?
The earlier app (10.79) was a standalone instrument, and also a plugin. It was an AUv3 Audio Unit instrument — confirmed loading and sounding in AUM on a device, with other plugin hosts untested. Playing an installed Audio Unit instrument on a Workstation track came in its last builds and was not confirmed on a device, so it is not claimed. Echoel Studio, in TestFlight now, has its own instruments as Audio Units too; they are not yet tested in a host app on a device. (A full list of what Echoelmusic deliberately does not claim is on this page.) The earlier app reached your DAW the open way instead — WAV export, plus live OSC / ADM-OSC and Art-Net / sACN output for stage rigs. (A virtual MIDI source existed but only appeared once you enabled a MIDI route in the routing panel. MIDI file export of the generated take shipped — in the export panel next to WAV. MPE output shipped too, behind two switches in the routing panel.) Versions for desktop music apps (CLAP, VST3) are a later plan, not before 2029, and are named here only once they run in a host.

Visuals & Lighting (earlier app)

What visual modes did it have?
GPU/Metal bio-reactive visuals shipped in it. EchoelVis rendered four generative looks — Rings, Water, Aurora and Depth — driven live by your heart rate, coherence and breath, in a movable floating window on the phone, or full-screen on a connected display (EchoelStage) for projection. Flash rate was capped under 3 Hz (WCAG epilepsy guidance) and Reduce Motion was honoured. Richer generative and AR worlds remained the wider vision.
Could it control lights and lasers?
Yes — the earlier app shipped Art-Net and sACN (E1.31) lighting output. Your heartbeat could drive professional fixtures (moving heads, LED bars) natively over open network standards — no vendor SDK, with flash safety built in. It also spoke OSC and ADM-OSC live, so your body could drive a TouchDesigner or Resolume rig in real time. Also live in it: EchoelStage external-display output (USB-C/HDMI/AirPlay — the visual took the connected screen). On its roadmap: DMX-512 interfaces, smart-home lights, projection mapping and a cue system. Echoel Studio sends Art-Net and sACN from its Connections page (in TestFlight, not yet checked on a device).

Video & Streaming (earlier app)

What video features did it have?
Video is not part of Echoelmusic 10.79, the earlier app. An NLE with a timeline, multi-cam, ProRes and color grading was built and then removed in July 2026, and recording the immersive visual to MP4 was removed in September 2026. What it shipped was audio and light: your heartbeat composed music you exported as WAV to Ableton, FL Studio or any DAW, or as a MIDI file; the immersive visual was rendered live, on the phone, on an external screen, or as Art-Net/sACN and OSC to your rig. Echoel Studio has video tracks (see the first questions on this page).
Could it stream live?
Live streaming (RTMP) did not ship in the earlier app. It was scoped and never built: the broadcast scaffold exists, its streaming library is not linked. For immersive and stage work, that app spoke open standards live — OSC, ADM-OSC (immersive audio objects), Art-Net and sACN (stage lighting) — so a performer's body could drive a TouchDesigner / Resolume / lighting rig in real time. Echoel Studio streams with Go Live (see the first questions on this page).

Intelligence (earlier app)

What AI features did it have?
None; on-device AI was on its roadmap. The vision for EchoelAI was entirely on-device via CoreML — AI stem separation to isolate vocals, bass and instruments, with no cloud and full privacy.

Collaboration & Immersive (earlier app)

Could I collaborate with others?
Yes — EchoelNet provided one-way OSC (Open Sound Control) output to DAWs, VJ software, and custom applications, plus a virtual MIDI 1.0 source any DAW could record live once a MIDI route was enabled — with MPE output over it once you turned on the two routing switches. OSC control input shipped too: a whitelist of /echoelmusic/ctrl/ cues (tempo while locked, key, scale, genre, visual look, blackout) plus ADM-OSC track positions (/adm/obj/n/) from a spatial controller, off by default. A full bidirectional OSC session and Ableton Link tempo sync were on the roadmap.
What about immersive and spatial setups?
The earlier app was iPhone-first. For immersive and spatial work it acted as a bio-reactive object source: the body (breath, coherence, HRV) drove an audio object's position over ADM-OSC — the open Audio Definition Model over Open Sound Control standard — into object-based renderers such as Adamson FletcherMachine, L-ISA and d&b Soundscape. No vendor SDK; open standards only. A dedicated Vision Pro app, on-device immersive scenes and other Apple platforms were on its roadmap — the Architecture page shows its status at the freeze.

Photosensitivity (earlier app)

Photosensitivity warnings?
The earlier app did not strobe. Every visual was capped below 3 Hz by construction (W3C WCAG photosensitivity guidance) and the cap could not be switched off; the system Reduce Motion setting was honoured. The same cap applied to Art-Net and sACN light output, so a rig driven by it inherited the cap. If you have photosensitive epilepsy or a history of seizures, please consult a physician before using bio-reactive visuals or light, and stop if you feel any discomfort — and when you drive a venue rig, remember the fixtures themselves can be bright: set safe levels for your audience.

Accessibility (earlier app)

Was the earlier app accessible?
It was built with accessibility in mind: VoiceOver labels on the primary controls, reduced-motion support, a dark high-contrast interface, and a safety cap that kept any visual movement at or below 3 Hz (W3C WCAG epilepsy guidance). It shipped in English. See our Accessibility and What we don’t claim pages.
