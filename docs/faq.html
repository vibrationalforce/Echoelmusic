FAQ

Echoelmusic is a music workstation for iPhone and iPad, in TestFlight and still being tested on devices.

What is Echoelmusic?
A music workstation for iPhone and iPad, in TestFlight and still being tested on devices: instruments, effects, a piano roll and a drum grid, an arrangement timeline, a session grid for live sets, audio and MIDI recording, mixing, automation and export. Songs are files on your device in an open, documented format, and the app needs no account.
How do I get it?
Echoelmusic is in TestFlight, not in the App Store. Request access by e-mail and name your device; you are put on the list, and the invitation comes by e-mail once the current build has passed its device tests. There is no public link yet.
Which devices does it run on?
iPhone and iPad with iOS 18 or later.
What does it cost?
The app is free: no ads, no tracking, no account. A one-time purchase for Echoelmusic’s own instruments, for use in other music apps, is planned; there is no subscription, and nothing is on sale yet.
Which instruments and effects does it have?
A 16-voice synth with eleven engines (Classic, Supersaw, FM, Sub, Morph, Mallet, DDSP, Choir, Pluck, Wavetable and Texture), a synthesized eight-piece drum kit with a step grid, and a sampler that plays an imported audio file across the keyboard. Twenty-five built-in effects, among them EQ, compressor, delay, reverb, pitch correction and a harmonizer, with up to eight on every track and on the master. Installed stereo Audio Unit instruments and effects from other apps play on tracks too.
Can I record, and what does it export?
Record a MIDI performance onto an armed instrument track, or mono audio from the microphone or one interface input onto an armed audio track; takes stay on the device. Export the arrangement as a 24-bit, 48 kHz stereo WAV with its loudness (LUFS) and true peak reported, as stems (one WAV per track, before the master effects), or as a Standard MIDI File (.mid).
Where are my songs, and who owns them?
Songs save automatically as .echoel files that the Files app shows, in an open, documented format. Everything you create with Echoelmusic belongs to you; the Terms say so in full.
Do its instruments and effects work in other apps?
Echoelmusic’s synth, drums, sampler and built-in effects are also Audio Units for other apps. They have not yet been tested in a host app on a device, and the instruments are not on sale yet.
Can it stream, send OSC or drive lights?
Yes, each off until you switch it on. The Connections page sends OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net and sACN out to your local network; body values are sent only while you allow body sharing on that page. Go Live streams the Field’s picture and the master mix over RTMP or RTMPS to a service you choose; nothing goes out until you press Go Live. None of this has been checked on a device yet.
Does Echoelmusic read my body?
Only if you choose a body source. A Bluetooth heart-rate chest strap can be a modulation source: heart rate, HRV or coherence on a fader, pan, send or synth parameter. Without a strap the control stays where you set it, and an export never reads the strap. The Privacy page lists every source the app can read and what happens to the values. Body values are for self-observation, not a medical diagnosis.
Is this a medical device?
No. Echoelmusic is a creative tool, not a medical device. Heart rate, HRV and coherence are used for creative control and self-observation only; they are not clinically validated measurements. See the Health Disclaimer.
Can the picture or connected lights flash?
Echoelmusic caps flashes in its own picture at three per second, and light sent over Art-Net or sACN is rate-limited as well. Venue rigs can still be bright and fast, so check any light rig before an audience. If you have photosensitive epilepsy or a history of seizures, consult a physician before use and stop at once if you feel discomfort. Details are on the Health page.
Is my data safe?
Your songs and takes stay on your device. No account, no analytics, no tracking or advertising SDKs, and no server of ours. What leaves the device, and only when you switch it on, is listed in the Privacy Policy; see also the Security page.
Is Echoelmusic accessible?
It has VoiceOver labels on the primary controls and a dark, high-contrast interface. What works and what is still missing is listed on the Accessibility page. The interface is in English.
