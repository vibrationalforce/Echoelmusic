Resolume: breathe on any parameter

Resolume learns an OSC address by watching what moves. Echoel sends a performer’s body out over OSC, one way, on open standards — so layer opacity, an effect mix or a clip’s speed can follow a breath or a settling pulse with nothing installed on either side.

1. Open the port in Resolume

  1. Preferences ▸ OSC → enable OSC input.
  2. Note the port Resolume is listening on. Echoel sends on 8000 by default; set either end so the two agree.

2. Point Echoel at the machine

  1. In Echoel: Routing ▸ OSC → host = the IP of the computer running Resolume, port = the one above.
  2. In the same panel, connect Body → OSC out. Nothing leaves the phone until that connection exists.
  3. Start a take and put a finger on the camera — or wear a Bluetooth strap, or let Apple Health feed it.

3. Teach a parameter the address

  1. Right-click any parameter in Resolume — layer opacity, an effect’s amount, a clip’s speed — and choose Edit OSC.
  2. Breathe. The address that moves is the one Resolume binds. That is the whole mapping step; there is no file to import and no template to keep in sync.

4. Which channels map straight — and the one that will not

A Resolume parameter runs from nought to one. Three body channels already do, so they land correctly with no scaling:

AddressRangeGood for
/echoelmusic/bio/breath/phase0–1Opacity, position, scale — it sweeps smoothly through every take and is the best first mapping.
/echoelmusic/bio/coherence0–1An effect mix or a blur that opens as the performer settles. Appears late — see below.
/echoelmusic/bio/heart/hrv0–1A slow second modulator alongside the breath.
/echoelmusic/bio/event/heartbeattriggerA bang per beat — bind it to a clip trigger or a flash rather than to a fader. Chest strap only: the camera pulse sends a rate, not per-beat bangs, so this address stays silent on a phone-only rig.

Heart rate is the exception, and it is worth knowing before you spend an evening on it. /echoelmusic/bio/heart/bpm carries beats per minute — roughly 40 to 200 — not a nought-to-one fraction. Bind it straight to a parameter and it pins at the top the moment the first value arrives, and stays there. Two ways round it: set an input range on the shortcut if your version of Resolume offers one (the band an actual body uses is far narrower than the address’s — something like 55 to 110), or leave the fader to breath/phase and give the pulse the event/heartbeat trigger instead, which is what it is good at.

5. Two behaviours that look like faults and are not

  • An address that never appears. Echoel sends a channel only while it is genuinely measured. A finger that slips off the camera sends nothing rather than a zero, and your last value simply holds — which is what you want mid-set, because a structural zero would pull a layer to black with no way to tell it from a performer who really stopped.
  • Coherence arrives last. It needs about sixteen accepted beats, so roughly sixteen seconds of a steady resting take. Map it to something that can afford to wait.

Echoel also sends what it is playing — the beat, the key, the level — on the same port; that is section 6 below. One more body address worth latching first: /echoelmusic/bio/synthetic is 1 while the built-in demo body is running and 0 once a real one takes over. Build and rehearse the whole patch on the demo, then watch it flip when the performer arrives.

6. The music, not only the body

Everything above is the performer’s physiology. Echoel also sends what it is playing, on the same port and the same tick — useful when you want a layer to follow the chord or the downbeat rather than the breath.

AddressRangeGood for
/echoelmusic/music/beat/phase0–1The musical pulse. Where breath/phase sweeps at the speed of a body, this one sweeps at the speed of the bar — the better choice for anything that should feel on the beat.
/echoelmusic/music/level/master0–1How loud the take is right now. Binds straight to opacity or a glow without scaling.
/echoelmusic/music/tempoBPMNot a fraction — the same caveat as heart rate above. Set an input range, or use it to drive a clip speed rather than a fader. It can be withheld — see below.
/echoelmusic/music/key/root0–11, or −1The key’s root as a pitch class, 0 = C. A colour per key is the obvious use. −1 means unknown, not “below the minimum” — treat it as a case, not as a value.
/echoelmusic/music/note/countwhole numberHow many notes sound right now. A density signal: thicker chord, busier layer. Also not a fraction.

One behaviour to know about before you build a patch on the tempo. /echoelmusic/music/tempo is the one musical address that can go quiet on purpose. In Flow the tempo follows the performer’s pulse, so it is a body reading wearing a musical name — and Echoel holds the same line for it that it holds for every body channel: readings taken from Apple Health, a Watch or a ring stay on the device and do not go out over the network. If the performer’s pulse is coming from one of those, the tempo address stays silent while the other four keep sending. A phone camera or a chest strap sends it normally, and so does a session with no biofeedback running at all. If your tempo fader never moves, that is the reason, and it is not a fault.

Also from the same phone

The same pulse can drive DMX fixtures over Art-Net or sACN and a spatial object over ADM-OSC at the same time as this feed — one body, several rigs. The full address list, the ports and the DMX channel map are on the integrations page; TouchDesigner and Reaper have their own walk-throughs.
