Boilerplate (long)

Echoelmusic is a music workstation for iPhone and iPad, built in Hamburg by the artist and developer Echoel (Michael Terbuyken). In TestFlight since 19 October 2026 and still being tested on devices, it holds instrument, audio and return tracks; a 16-voice synth with eleven engines (Classic, Supersaw, FM, Sub, Morph, Mallet, DDSP, Choir, Pluck, Wavetable and Texture), a synthesized eight-piece drum kit and a sampler; a piano roll, a drum step grid, an arrangement timeline and a session grid that launches clips and scenes on the next bar; MIDI and mono audio recording; a mixer with sends, automation and up to eight effects on every track and the master; and export as a 24-bit, 48 kHz WAV with its loudness and true peak reported, as stems, or as a Standard MIDI file (.mid). Songs are open, documented .echoel files on the device.

A Bluetooth heart-rate chest strap can be a modulation source: heart rate, HRV or coherence moves a fader, pan, send or synth parameter the player routes it to — a control signal, presented for self-observation, never as a medical diagnosis. Also in TestFlight and not yet checked on a device: a Connections page that sends OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net and sACN to the local network, each off until switched on, and Go Live, which streams the Field’s picture and the master mix over RTMP or RTMPS to a service the player chooses. The app is free, with no account, no ads and no tracking.

Until October 2026 TestFlight carried an earlier app (10.79, now frozen), an instrument played by the body. It is documented in the archive; its parts return to Echoelmusic in the order on the Roadmap.
