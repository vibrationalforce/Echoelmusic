Press Kit

Facts, boilerplate and assets for writing about Echoel and Echoel Studio

One-liner

Echoel Studio is a music workstation for iPhone and iPad: write, arrange, record and mix songs with eleven synth engines, a drum kit and a sampler, then export WAV, stems or MIDI. In TestFlight, free, with no account.

Boilerplate (short)

Echoel Studio is a music workstation for iPhone and iPad, in TestFlight and still being tested on devices. It has a 16-voice synth with eleven engines, a synthesized drum kit and a sampler, a piano roll and a drum step grid, an arrangement timeline and a session grid, MIDI and audio recording, a mixer with sends and automation, and export as WAV, stems or a MIDI file. Songs are files on the device in an open, documented format; there is no account, no ads and no tracking. Echoel is built in Hamburg by artist and developer Echoel (Michael Terbuyken).

Boilerplate (long)

Echoel — Physical Computing · Biofeedback · Multimedial & Multidimensional — is building a music workstation for iPhone and iPad. Echoel Studio, in TestFlight since 19 October 2026 and still being tested on devices, holds instrument, audio and return tracks; a 16-voice synth with eleven engines (Classic, Supersaw, FM, Sub, Morph, Mallet, DDSP, Choir, Pluck, Wavetable and Texture), a synthesized eight-piece drum kit and a sampler; a piano roll, a drum step grid, an arrangement timeline and a session grid that launches clips and scenes on the next bar; MIDI and mono audio recording; a mixer with sends, automation and up to eight effects on every track and the master; and export as a 24-bit, 48 kHz WAV with its loudness and true peak reported, as stems, or as a Standard MIDI file (.mid). Songs are open, documented .echoel files on the device.

A Bluetooth heart-rate chest strap can be a modulation source: heart rate, HRV or coherence moves a fader, pan, send or synth parameter the player routes it to — a control signal, presented for self-observation, never as a medical diagnosis. Also in TestFlight and not yet checked on a device: a Connections page that sends OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net and sACN to the local network, each off until switched on, and Go Live, which streams the Field’s picture and the master mix over RTMP or RTMPS to a service the player chooses. The app is free, with no account, no ads and no tracking.

Before Echoel Studio, the earlier app (10.79, now frozen) was an instrument played by the body: a fingertip on the camera measured the pulse, and the body’s signals composed music, visuals and light in real time. Its parts return to Echoel Studio in the order on the Roadmap.

What the earlier app did (10.79, frozen)

  • Pulse from the camera (fingertip photoplethysmography), any standard Bluetooth heart-rate strap (Polar, Wahoo, Garmin and more), or Apple Health — plus an instant demo mode.
  • Real HRV coherence from research-grade frequency analysis (Lomb-Scargle, Welch) — numbers first, always visible.
  • One-tap generative composition: forty-one curated genres, 57 scales, your key, concert pitch A4 freely settable 380–500 Hz, tempo locked or flowing with the heart.
  • A real instrument: polyphonic synth with a live patch editor and preset library, sub-bass voice, loop cutter, production FX chain.
  • Studio handoff: stamped WAV export (artist · date · key · BPM · tuning) and MIDI file export (.mid, carrying tempo, 4/4 and the key signature, spanning whole bars). Live MIDI: input from an external controller including pitch bend and CC 74, and note output to your rig (MIDI 1.0, switched on in the routing panel), including MPE output — two further switches spread the notes over member channels 2–16 with Glide, Slide and Press per note. MPE input zone handling was on the roadmap, not in that build.
  • Immersive GPU visuals, flash-safe (≤3 Hz) and Reduce-Motion aware, on the phone or on an external screen.
  • Open-standard stage I/O: OSC, ADM-OSC immersive objects, Art-Net and sACN lighting.
  • Nearby session sharing between two iPhones (peer-to-peer, no server).

Not in the earlier app: video editing, audio recording (it had no microphone input) and live broadcast — video editing was built and then removed in July 2026, and live broadcast was never built. Its last builds added a Workstation that arranged parts on tracks, with a note editor and .mid and audio import; playing installed Audio Unit instruments on a Workstation track was never confirmed on a device, so it is not claimed. The Architecture page records that app’s status at the freeze.

Story angles

  • A full workstation on a phone: instruments, a piano roll and a drum grid, an arrangement timeline and a session grid, recording, mixing and export on iPhone and iPad — free, with no account.
  • The body as a modulation source: a Bluetooth heart-rate strap moves a fader, a send or a synth parameter the player routes it to — described as a mechanism, never as an effect on the player.
  • Science, not wellness: peer-reviewed bio-signal processing (HRV resonance research) as a creative modulation source, presented for self-observation, never as therapy.
  • Open standards on stage: OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net and sACN out from one phone, each off until switched on — in TestFlight, not yet checked on a device.
  • Privacy as a feature: a music workstation with no account, no cloud, no analytics and no tracking SDKs; songs are open files on the device.
  • Solo-built in Hamburg: an independent artist-developer building a full music workstation.
