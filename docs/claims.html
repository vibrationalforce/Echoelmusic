What we do not claim

Most software tells you what it does. This page tells you what we deliberately do not claim: first for Echoelmusic as it is in TestFlight today, then thirteen entries about the earlier app (10.79), each with the reason. It is the public half of a register we keep internally.

Echoelmusic today (in TestFlight)

Echoelmusic is a music workstation for iPhone and iPad. What the build holds is on the Roadmap. This is what we do not claim for it:

  • Not in the App Store. There is TestFlight only, and no store link or badge until the first release is approved.
  • Not finished. The build is in TestFlight and still being tested on devices; we call nothing in it proven until a device has shown it.
  • Not “works in AUM, Logic Pro or GarageBand”. Its own instruments and effects exist as Audio Units, but none has been run in a host app on a device yet.
  • Nothing to buy: no price, no “Pro”, no trial. The app is free. A one-time purchase for its own instrument plugins is planned and not yet on sale; there is no subscription.
  • Not mastering, and not a normalised export. The export measures loudness and true peak and reports them; it does not change the level for you.
  • Not an AI that writes your song. The assistant appears only on devices with Apple Intelligence. It changes tempo, tracks, sounds, the mix and simple patterns when you ask; it does not compose, and it has not been run on a device yet.
  • Not “Autotune”, voice cloning or an AI voice. Pitch correction is an effect on a track, nothing more.
  • Not a health product. A heart-rate strap is a modulation source: we describe what it moves, never an effect on you, and nothing here is a medical device or a diagnosis.

Why a page like this exists

Echoelmusic is built by one person, in the open, and it changes fast. Twice we shipped copy describing a feature that had been removed — once on this website, once in an App Store listing. Both took a full cycle to undo. So we started writing down what is not true, next to why, and we check the website against that list automatically. If a sentence anywhere contradicts this page, this page is the one that is right.

The earlier app (10.79): thirteen things it did not claim

The entries below describe the earlier Echoelmusic app (10.79), an instrument played by the body, which TestFlight carried until October 2026 and which is frozen. None of them describes Echoelmusic as it is today, and several read the other way round for it: today’s app records audio onto a track you arm, has drums and a sampler, takes notes and chords from a MIDI keyboard, and links one third-party package for the stream that starts when you press Go Live.

Where it ran

It was an Audio Unit — and we claimed only the host we tested

The earlier app (10.79) was an AUv3 instrument, and it was confirmed loading and sounding in AUM on a device. Logic Pro, GarageBand and Ableton Live were never tested, and we claimed none of them. One host is one host. In the other direction, loading other people’s instrument plugins inside it was in testing and not confirmed on a device, so we did not claim it.

The Apple Watch did not measure for it, and did not control it

In the earlier app (10.79), the watch app was a display. It read what the iPhone wrote; it did not send a pulse back, and the transport for that direction was never built. Watch heart rate also arrives roughly four to five seconds late — usable for a trend, never for beat sync. That app’s real sensor was a finger on the phone camera.

It is not in the App Store yet

The earlier app (10.79) was never released on the App Store, and Echoelmusic is not there yet either: today there is TestFlight only. Any “download on the App Store” badge or store link you see elsewhere is premature, and we will change this line on the day the first release is approved — not before.

What it was not

Not wellness, meditation, sleep, focus, stress relief, longevity or “HealthTech”

In the earlier app (10.79), biofeedback was a control signal, the way a keyboard or a breath controller is. We described the mechanism — a calmer pulse made different music — and never an effect on you. We did not claim it calmed you, helped you sleep, or improved any health marker. The app itself said its data was for self-observation, not medical diagnosis, and the marketing was not allowed to say something the app contradicted. The same rule holds for Echoelmusic today.

Not “biohacking”

The earlier app (10.79) was not “biohacking” either: same reason as above, and the word gets its own line because it once slipped past that one. It is the word that files an instrument under self-optimisation culture, which is exactly where this one did not belong. It appears in no script, caption, hashtag or title, then or now.

It did not post anything anywhere

The earlier app (10.79) had no social-media integration. It did not publish to TikTok, YouTube or Instagram, automatically or otherwise, and there was no AI agent or tool protocol running inside the app. What you exported, you exported yourself.

Features that were gone, or were never there

No drums, video editing, livestream or audio recorder

In the earlier app (10.79), all of these had either been removed or were never wired up, and none was claimed. It had no drum machine, no video capture or editing, no livestreaming (RTMP was scoped and never built), and no audio recorder — it had no microphone input. What its Workstation did have was new and still being tested on devices when the app was frozen: it arranged parts on tracks, mixed them (level, pan, mute, solo), imported .mid and audio files, loaded one sample per track, edited MIDI notes and recorded MIDI takes from a controller. It recorded MIDI, never sound. Until October 2026 this entry also denied a “sampler instrument”; the last builds had a sampler per track, so that denial was dropped.

No microphone: no voice recording, cloning, AI voice, text-to-speech or pitch correction

In the earlier app (10.79), the audio input path had been removed entirely. Nothing in that app listened. It had no pitch correction on your voice, no harmoniser, no vocoder, no voice model of any kind. What it recorded was its own output.

No subscription, no paywall, nothing to buy

The earlier app (10.79) was free and complete, and it showed no purchase screen. There was no “Pro” tier to buy, no monthly or annual access, and no recurring revenue to report. That is still so for Echoelmusic today: the app is free, there is no subscription, and the one-time purchase planned for its own instrument plugins is not on sale yet.

Precision about what the body actually drove

Breath depth and LF/HF were measured, but they did not shape the sound

In the earlier app (10.79), both were pinned to a neutral value everywhere they reached the synthesis engine, so neither changed what you heard — and both sound impressive in a caption, which is precisely why they are listed here. What was real: coherence, heart rate, heart-rate variability and breath phase each drove audible parameters, and the coherence trend morphed the timbre as it rose or fell.

MPE went out, not in

The earlier app (10.79) could send MPE to your rig, as a switch you turned on. It did not receive MPE: the input parsed the traffic but did not distinguish zones, so per-note expression arriving from a controller was not separated per note. Pitch bend, pressure and slide all sounded — on one voice.

An external controller played the body voice — singular

In the earlier app (10.79), a plugged-in MIDI controller sounded immediately, ahead of the breath envelope. But exactly one voice consumed those events and it was monophonic. We did not say “plays the voices”, “play chords” or “polyphonic” about that path.

What it was built on

Zero external dependencies — so also zero of the usual suspects

The earlier app (10.79) shipped with no third-party packages at all. That meant no Composable Architecture, no RevenueCat, no SwiftData, no VideoToolbox and no HaishinKit — several of which had been attributed to it. It was Swift, SwiftUI, and Apple frameworks, plus open standards on the wire: OSC, ADM-OSC, Art-Net, sACN and MIDI. Echoelmusic today links one package, HaishinKit, for the stream that starts when you press Go Live.
