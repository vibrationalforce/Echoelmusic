The earlier instrument (10.79)

Before Echoelmusic there was an instrument played by the body: a camera pulse reading, generative visuals and light. It is frozen at version 10.79.515 and no longer in TestFlight. The sections below describe it as it was; its parts return to Echoelmusic in the order on the Roadmap.

Heartbeat to art form

One signal chain: your biology was the instrument, the score and the conductor.

01

Sense

The iPhone camera or a Bluetooth heart-rate strap read your heartbeat in real time; the camera also estimated your breathing. Apple Health data (Watch, ring) joined with a few seconds’ delay. The camera needed no setup.

02

Transform

Biology shaped sound, light and visuals. Coherence opened the mix. Your pulse set the tempo until you locked it.

03

Radiate

Takes went to your DAW, your pulse to a venue’s lights and spatial rig, and you felt it through a sub. From living room to festival stage.

The science, measured — not mystified

Biofeedback as a real signal you can see. Shown, never promised.

Measured

RMSSD, SDNN and pNN50 from your beat-to-beat intervals — the standard time-domain HRV metrics, computed on device from the camera or a Bluetooth strap. The earlier app did not read Apple Health’s beat-to-beat series, so with a Watch you got Apple’s own SDNN.

Frequency-domain coherence

A real spectrum of your heartbeat (Lomb–Scargle / Welch) read in the standard HRV bands. Coherence is the share of power gathered in the ~0.1 Hz resonance peak — a number, not a vibe.

Honest limits

A chest strap (e.g. Polar H10) is ground truth; wrist and camera readings are estimates. This is for self-observation — not medical diagnosis, and no “healing frequencies”.

One body, many dimensions.

Sound, space, light and vibration — played live by your physiology, over open standards, in the earlier app.

Your body was the controller

Every heartbeat and breath shaped sound and light in real time. A MIDI controller could play one voice on top.

Bio-generative music

Harmonic, melodic and rhythmic loops in any key and BPM, composed by your heartbeat and breath; a take exported to .wav or to a MIDI file (.mid) dropped straight into Logic Pro, Ableton or FL Studio.

Living visuals

A Metal GPU bio-renderer painted with your body in four generative looks — heart rate to pulse, the sounding tone to hue, breath to spread, coherence to sharpness — in a movable floating window on the phone, or full-screen on a connected display for projection. The picture was playable: touch the visual and your fingers become notes, quantized to the take’s key. A screen connected over USB-C/HDMI or AirPlay became its own stage while the phone stayed the instrument. Flash rate held under 3 Hz for safety.

Open spine

The body went anywhere it needed to go: OSC, MIDI 2.0-ready in and MIDI 1.0 out with MPE note output, ADM-OSC immersive objects, Art-Net and sACN. MPE input zones, Ableton Link and on-device AI were on that app’s roadmap; it never had video editing or live streaming. Echoelmusic has video tracks and a live stream that starts only when you press Go Live (in TestFlight, not yet checked on a device).

Your pulse became light

Your body streamed to stage and venue rigs over open lighting standards — native Art-Net and sACN, no vendor SDK. Your heartbeat drove the room, with flash safety built in.

What the earlier app did

The summary the press kit carried until 9 November 2026.

  • Pulse from the camera (fingertip photoplethysmography), any standard Bluetooth heart-rate strap (Polar, Wahoo, Garmin and more), or Apple Health — plus an instant demo mode.
  • Real HRV coherence from research-grade frequency analysis (Lomb-Scargle, Welch) — numbers first, always visible.
  • One-tap generative composition: forty-one curated genres, 57 scales, your key, concert pitch A4 freely settable 380–500 Hz, tempo locked or flowing with the heart.
  • A real instrument: polyphonic synth with a live patch editor and preset library, sub-bass voice, loop cutter, production FX chain.
  • Studio handoff: stamped WAV export (artist · date · key · BPM · tuning) and MIDI file export (.mid, carrying tempo, 4/4 and the key signature, spanning whole bars). Live MIDI: input from an external controller including pitch bend and CC 74, and note output to your rig (MIDI 1.0, switched on in the routing panel), including MPE output — two further switches spread the notes over member channels 2–16 with Glide, Slide and Press per note. MPE input zone handling was on the roadmap, not in that build.
  • Immersive GPU visuals, flash-safe (≤3 Hz) and Reduce-Motion aware, on the phone or on an external screen.
  • Open-standard stage I/O: OSC, ADM-OSC immersive objects, Art-Net and sACN lighting.
  • Nearby session sharing between two iPhones (peer-to-peer, no server).

Not in the earlier app: video editing, audio recording (it had no microphone input) and live broadcast — video editing was built and then removed in July 2026, and live broadcast was never built. Its last builds added a Workstation that arranged parts on tracks, with a note editor and .mid and audio import; playing installed Audio Unit instruments on a Workstation track was never confirmed on a device, so it is not claimed. The Architecture page records that app’s status at the freeze.
