# Biosignal-Driven Generative Music, Visuals and Light (2018–2026): Evidence Brief for Echoelmusic

Scope note: research conducted 2026-10-16 with web search only (about 14 calls). Many findings rest on abstracts and search snippets, not full texts; they are flagged where that matters. Peer-reviewed findings and marketing claims are kept apart throughout.

## Academic work on biofeedback / physiologically-adaptive generative music: what exists and what does the evidence say?

### Takeaway
Closed-loop, physiology-adaptive *generative* music is still a small, early field: proof-of-concept systems (NIME/arXiv) and small or student studies, with mixed results. The strongest peer-reviewed findings are *around* the loop, not inside it: audiences synchronise physiologically at live concerts; HRV biofeedback (paced/resonance breathing) has a large review literature; and the popular idea that heart rate entrains to musical tempo is weakly supported.

### Cited Findings
- **iHeartLift (Ho & Chen, EMBC 2011):** a closed-loop biofeedback system that compares a song's tempo variability with the listener's current HRV and picks music meant to move HRV in a target direction. It selects existing music; it does not generate it. — [dblp record](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/embc/HoC11.html)
- **Dartmouth master's thesis (2025), closed-loop biofeedback + AI-generated music:** modest HRV improvements in the biofeedback conditions. Participants reported emotional limitations (short tracks, abrupt transitions). One condition with four AI tracks gave a *significant HRV decrease*. Calming AI tracks produced stronger parasympathetic activation than energising ones. The authors conclude deeper personalisation is needed. This is a thesis, not a peer-reviewed paper. — [Dartmouth Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.dartmouth.edu/masters_theses/205)
- **Easthope, "Coupling the Heart to Musical Machines" (arXiv 2505.03073, 2025):** a NIME-style controller. PPG heart rate from a Polar Verity Sense, streamed over BLE at 55 Hz, is the single control parameter and time-scales audio buffers, which closes a biofeedback loop. The author proposes generative latent spaces to extend this one-dimensional control. It is a proof of concept with no formal evaluation, and the venue (NIME or not) was not confirmed. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03073v1)
- **Jaimovich & Knapp (NIME 2015):** heart-rate and EDA algorithms for musical use, built from a physiological database of 4,000+ people. The paper explicitly addresses how calibration varies between individuals, which is directly relevant to personalisation. — [NIME proceedings](https://nime.org/proc/nime2015_jjaimovich/)
- **EEG brain-computer musical interface (arXiv 2606.01473, 2026):** maps continuous valence estimates to musical mode, tempo and rhythmic density. N=22 evaluation, and it reports null findings where they occurred. It shows the honest-reporting norm in affective sonification. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01473)
- **Cambridge (2026), "body–computer interfaces":** turns harmonic patterns in biological rhythms (heartbeats and others) into tunings and chord progressions. Only the abstract or snippet was seen. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D972D4491749CA2173C7863BBCF20145)
- **ISCAS 2011 valence/arousal drum-loop model:** links rhythm to HRV in 22 healthy subjects and reports that rhythms can be synthesised for "relaxing" vs "exciting". It is small and older. — reported in search summary alongside [NYCU repository record](https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/15157?locale=en)
- **Tempo→heart-rate entrainment is weakly supported.** In a 2024 *Psychology of Music* study, 36 listeners heard tempo changes set relative to their own current heart rate. There was "little evidence of entrainment": every tempo change, in either direction and of any size, produced a similar small rise in heart rate and arousal, which fits a general arousal effect of rhythm. The paper notes earlier results were mixed, possibly because of methodological problems. — [SAGE, Psychology of Music](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03057356241302809)
- An older line of work (Bernardi et al., summarised in a 2005 *Heart* editorial) found that breathing rate rose in proportion to music tempo, and so did heart rate and blood pressure. It leaves open whether this is direct entrainment or a respiratory effect. — [PMC (Heart editorial)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1860853)
- **Audience physiological synchrony is real in concert settings.** Tschacher et al. (*Scientific Reports*, Oct 2023) measured 132 audience members across three classical concerts. They found synchrony in heart rate, respiration rate, skin conductance and movement, but *not* in breathing timing. Heart-rate synchrony was higher when listeners felt moved, inspired or immersed. — [DOAJ / Sci Rep](https://doaj.org/article/22947de171a8455e851f349b63c59c8a); press: [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/2023/11/harmony-in-heartbeats-berlin-concert-study-reveals-synchrony-in-classical-music-listeners-214782)
- A companion 2023 paper (*Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*) linked skin-conductance and respiration-rate synchrony to self-reported appreciation. A Japanese grant project (KAKEN, preliminary) found no temporal heart-rate synchrony between performers and audience, but audience emotional impact did track the performers' heart rates. — [experimental-concert-research.org](https://experimental-concert-research.org/?p=552); [KAKEN](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-21K19734)
- **HRV biofeedback (HRVB) review base:** Lehrer et al. 2020 (*Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback* 45(3):109–129, DOI 10.1007/s10484-020-09466-z) screened 1,868 papers and included 58, covering clinical symptoms plus athletic, cognitive and artistic performance. Protocols differ: individual resonance frequency, device-guided pacing, and "preset pace" at about 6 breaths/min. Pooled effect sizes were not retrieved. — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10412682); [AAPB PDF](https://aapb.starchapter.com/images/meta_analysis_andstatus_of_field_.pdf); [BioSource on resonance frequency](https://biosourcesoftware.com/post/rethinking-the-resonance-frequency-rf-part-1)
- Laborde et al. (*Psychophysiology*, 2021) studied slow-paced breathing at 6 cycles/min, with or without biofeedback. It is the relevant reference for whether the breathing itself or the feedback display does the work. Only the title was seen. — [Bournemouth eprints PDF](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/38163/7/Psychophysiology%20-%202021%20-%20Laborde%20-%20Psychophysiological%20effects%20of%20slow%E2%80%90paced%20breathing%20at%20six%20cycles%20per%20minute%20with%20or.pdf)
- **HRV methodology caution:** a 2014 *Frontiers* review of short-term frequency-domain HRV ("Everything Hertz") criticises how LF power is interpreted, the use of LFnu/HFnu and LF/HF as if they were independent measures, and the poor control of extraneous variables. It calls for general methodological improvement. — [Frontiers in Physiology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2014.00177/full); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4019878/)
- **"Coherence" as a score:** HeartMath's coherence is the power in the peak around ~0.1 Hz relative to the rest of the spectrum. The score also depends on a user-set "challenge level". No independent peer-reviewed validation of this ratio against outcomes turned up, and the HeartMath-linked studies found were small (n=13, n=22) and not independent. — [HeartCloud docs](https://heartcloud.com/library/challenge_levels.html); [IDEAS/RePEc study](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ibn/gjhsjl/v10y2018i2p126.html)

### Inferences
- Echoelmusic's design choice, "your heartbeat is NOT the beat" (body tempo folded and slew-limited, never phase-locked), is **consistent with the evidence**. Tempo→HR entrainment is not established, so a product claim like "the music slows your heart" would be an overclaim. A claim like "the music follows your body" is defensible, because it describes system behaviour rather than a physiological effect.
- The defensible scientific frame for Echoelmusic is **sonification and performance control**, not intervention outcomes. The audience-synchrony literature (Tschacher 2023) supports an artistic/installation framing: physiology is a meaningful, shared, observable signal in live settings. It says nothing about health benefit.
- "Coherence" should be presented as a defined spectral measure (and the app's own definition documented), not as a validated state. LF/HF in particular should not be sold as a "balance" readout, given the 2014 methodology critique. This matches the repo's existing decision not to claim the LF/HF mapping.
- The Dartmouth result (abrupt transitions hurt the experience; one AI condition *decreased* HRV) is a design warning. Generative transitions and continuity matter more than "AI-ness", and adaptive music can move physiology in the unintended direction.
- Novelty gap: no peer-reviewed system was found that does *generative composition* (key/scale/harmony) conditioned live on HR/HRV/breath *and* fans the same body signal out to light (DMX) and spatial objects (ADM-OSC). That combination appears unclaimed in the literature.

### Gaps
- No pooled effect sizes or GRADE-style evidence ratings were retrieved for Lehrer 2020, Goessl 2017 (stress/anxiety) or Pizzoli 2021 (depression). The figure "g = 0.81" circulates only on a low-reliability aggregator and is deliberately not reported here.
- No ICMC/ISMIR/CHI paper on HR/breath-conditioned generative composition specifically was found in this pass. A targeted search (CHI "biofeedback music generation", ISMIR "physiological conditioning") is still needed.
- The 2026 King's College London ESC abstract (126 participants, piano tracks, RR data) appeared only as a snippet, and no full paper was found. — [KCL cosmos](https://cosmos.isd.kcl.ac.uk/?p=7958)

## Commercial and artistic precedents: claims vs evidence

### Takeaway
Commercial "adaptive/functional music" leans on one or two company-linked studies. Brain.fm has the one genuinely peer-reviewed mechanism paper (*Communications Biology* 2024), but it concerns amplitude modulation for attention, not biosignal adaptation. Endel's headline "7x focus" rests on an industry study. Wavepaths is a practitioner-steered generative system with no published outcome trial found. The artistic precedent most relevant to Echoelmusic is Lozano-Hemmer's heart-rate-driven light installations.

### Cited Findings
- **Endel:** markets personalised soundscapes. Its headline "up to 7x focus" comes from a study run by **Arctop** (a neurotech/AI company) comparing Endel Focus with focus playlists and silence over four one-hour sessions. Endel calls it peer-reviewed but dates it inconsistently (2021 vs 2022). Further claims ("3.6x less stress", "95% consistent focus", Wind Down "designed to lower your cortisol") were not found to have peer-reviewed support. — [Endel science page](https://www.endel.io/science); [Endel blog](https://endel.io/blog/the-science-behind-focus); [press clipping](https://endel.rockpaperscissors.biz/dispatch/pu/28284)
- **Brain.fm:** Woods et al., "Rapid modulation in music supports attention in listeners with attentional difficulties", *Communications Biology*, Oct 2024 (DOI 10.1038/s42003-024-07026-3), Northeastern MIND Lab. Music with added amplitude modulation sustained attention better than slow-modulation music or pink noise. Beta-range modulation helped participants with more ADHD symptoms more. fMRI showed greater attentional-network activity and EEG showed greater stimulus–brain coupling. The music was supplied by Brain.fm, and Brain.fm's own blog goes beyond the abstract (e.g. a "16 Hz" emphasis). It is evidence for sustained attention in people with attentional difficulties, not a clinical treatment. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/cb3004de45b74c76948f0a15a79e29e1); [ADDitude summary](https://www.additudemag.com/background-music-amplitude-modulation-adhd-study/); marketing: [Brain.fm blog](https://www.brain.fm/blog/focus-music-for-adhd)
- **Wavepaths:** generative music assembled from artist-composed stems according to controllable variables. The *practitioner*, not a sensor, steers emotional tone, depth, intensity and instrumentation live. The company raised a $4.5M seed round for personalisation and music generation. Its 2022 partnership with Entheon (EBIQ-101, an observational ketamine study at Heading Health) set out to correlate musical structure with EEG. No published results were found, and no psilocybin-specific trial was found. — [Newsfile release](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/113958/Entheon-Biomedical-Partners-with-Wavepaths-on-Upcoming-Observational-Study); [Wavepaths funding](https://wavepaths.com/blog/wavepaths-raises-4-5-million); [Wavepaths product page](https://wavepaths.com/music-for-psychedelic-therapy)
- **Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Room* (2006):** about 300 incandescent bulbs on voltage controllers, driven by a heart-rate sensor near the entrance. Each visitor's pulse goes to one bulb and blinks in their rhythm. The last 300 pulses are held and shifted along the grid, producing asynchronous light. It was shown at the Venice Biennale 2007 and in the Hirshhorn's 2018–19 "Pulse" exhibition, with "Pulse Index" and "Pulse Tank" and with heartbeat audio added. The artist publishes a technical manual. — [Hirshhorn](https://hirshhorn.si.edu/exhibitions/rafael-lozano-hemmer-pulse); [MACM](https://macm.org/en/collections/oeuvre/pulse-room/); [artist manual PDF](https://www.lozano-hemmer.com/texts/manuals/pulse_room.pdf)
- **BITalino (PLUX):** a low-cost, modular, open-source biosignal kit (ECG, EDA, EMG and others, BLE streaming, OpenSignals visualiser, APIs). Its intended users explicitly include artists and performers. Academic papers: SciTePress 2013 and 2014. No documented OSC bridge was found in this pass. — [SciTePress 2014](https://www.scitepress.org/Documents/2014/47278/); [PLUX HomeBIT](https://www.pluxbiosignals.com/products/homebit)

### Inferences
- **Positioning implication:** Endel and Brain.fm sell *outcomes* (focus, sleep, stress). Wavepaths sells a *practitioner tool*. Echoelmusic's honest lane is the third one, an *instrument*: the body as a controller whose effect is audible, visible and inspectable. Echoelmusic should not borrow outcome language such as "focus", "lower cortisol" or "reduce stress". Even the best-evidenced competitor claim (Brain.fm) is narrow and stimulus-specific, not biosignal-driven.
- Wavepaths shows a **"human-in-the-loop steering"** pattern (the practitioner moves high-level variables over stem-based generation). This maps well onto Echoelmusic's Loop mode (user-set) next to Flow mode (body-following).
- *Pulse Room* is the canonical precedent for **heart → light** and also an early example of **flash-rate risk**: pulse-driven blinking bulbs. Echoelmusic's 3 Hz ceiling on visual flashes is aligned with WCAG (see the on-device visuals question below). Pulse rate itself sits below 3 Hz up to 180 bpm.

### Gaps
- **Myndstream, Muse, TouchDesigner/Resolume/MadMapper/grandMA practice:** this pass found no citable sources for them, so nothing is claimed about them. Muse EEG validity and its "adaptive soundscape" evidence need a separate search.
- The Arctop/Endel study's journal, authors, sample size and effect sizes were not retrieved, so it cannot be graded.
- No living artist doing biosignal live performance (beyond Lozano-Hemmer) was retrieved with sources in this pass.

## Machine learning on the body→music mapping: personalisation, RL, emotion recognition, and overclaiming risk

### Takeaway
Learned or personalised mappings are a credible research direction, but the field is preprint-heavy. No found system combines RL with live physiology to drive a *generative* music model. Emotion recognition from HRV/PPG degrades sharply outside the lab, and reporting is often too poor to compare studies. "Generative intelligence" in Echoelmusic should therefore model *signal quality, individual baselines and user preference*, not "detect emotions".

### Cited Findings
- **BREATH (Xiaomi, arXiv 2510.15895, 2025):** mm-wave radar measures HR and respiration without contact. An LLM-based reasoning agent maps them to musical descriptors that condition a diffusion audio model. There is no RL, and it is a preprint. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.15895)
- **NRRS (Hong et al., ACM 2020):** wireless vital-sign sensing during listening, with RL adapting *recommendations* of existing tracks rather than generating music. — [ECNU Pure record](https://pure.ecnu.edu.cn/en/publications/nonintrusive-sensing-and-reinforcement-learning-based-adaptive-pe/)
- An IJIRT paper proposes RL-refined generation from wearable biometrics with a thin evaluation, so it is weak evidence. — [IJIRT PDF](https://ijirt.org/publishedpaper/IJIRT206820_PAPER.pdf)
- A *Frontiers in Computer Science* 2025 article on adaptive music and physiology appeared in the results, but its content was not reviewed. — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1593905/pdf)
- **Emotion recognition, systematic review (Bulbul et al., IEEE Trans. Instrumentation & Measurement, 2026):** 549 papers screened, 48 ML studies (2019–2025) using wearable ECG/GSR. Essential measurement details are frequently missing: electrode type and placement, skin–electrode impedance, signal-quality indices, calibration, repeatability. This prevents attributing performance to sensing vs modelling and complicates comparison across studies. — [UTU repository](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/526591462)
- In daily life, emotion detection performs worse than in semi-restricted or lab settings, and individual physiology, activity level and activity type affect accuracy. — [UGM survey](https://ir.lib.ugm.ac.id/id/eprint/8601/)
- HRV is considered more useful for **stress/arousal** monitoring than fine-grained emotion labels. — ["Current Challenges of Using Wearable Devices for Online Emotion Sensing", arXiv 2208.05206](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.05206); see also [Saganowski et al., arXiv 1912.10528](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.10528)
- **Sensor validity (relevant to rPPG):** fingertip and facial smartphone PPG vs 12-lead ECG (Yan et al. 2017, n=40, iPhone rear camera) is reasonable for heart rate at rest and after exercise. — [JMIR mHealth / PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5368348/)
- Smartphone-PPG **HRV** (lnRMSSD) was validated against ECG around resistance exercise (n=31) in a 2020 study. Agreement statistics were not retrieved. — [PMC7600564](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7600564)
- A 2026 meta-analysis protocol cites De Ridder et al.: smartphone PPG agrees with ECG/oximetry but varies significantly with the sample, environment and phone hardware. — [JMIR Res Protoc 2026](https://www.researchprotocols.org/2026/1/e84837/PDF)
- A vendor (Welltory) claims camera readings are "just as accurate" as HR monitors. This is a marketing claim, and the same page concedes it is no substitute for ECG. — [Welltory help](https://help.welltory.com/articles/3352614)
- Individual calibration of HR/EDA mapping is an explicit NIME concern (Jaimovich & Knapp 2015). — [NIME](https://nime.org/proc/nime2015_jjaimovich/)

### Inferences
Where a "generative intelligence" layer can credibly make the mapping smarter, ranked by defensibility:

1. **Per-user baselines and normalisation**: map deviation from *your own* rolling baseline, not absolute bpm or ms. This is supported by the calibration literature and makes no claim about inner states.
2. **Signal-quality-aware mapping**: weight or freeze modulation by a confidence index. Bulbul 2026 shows signal quality is the field's blind spot, and hardware variance is the main smartphone-PPG caveat. Echoelmusic already gates on rPPG confidence. Making confidence a *first-class modulation input* (e.g. crossfading toward a neutral value) is cheap and honest.
3. **Preference learning on the musical side**: learn which genre, density or harmony responses *the user keeps or skips* (explicit feedback, a contextual bandit). It is on-device, needs no emotion label, and is reversible. A bandit is a much smaller claim than an "RL closed loop on your physiology".
4. **Bounded closed-loop targets**: e.g. matching music density to breath rate, *as a performance behaviour*. Avoid optimising toward a physiological target ("raise HRV"). The Dartmouth thesis shows it can backfire, and it turns the product into an intervention claim.
5. **Avoid entirely in user-facing copy**: "detects your emotion/mood", "knows how you feel", "stress detection". The accuracy and reporting limits make these overclaims, and arousal-ish proxies are the most HRV can support.
- An LLM/descriptor-conditioning pattern like BREATH (body → descriptors → generator) is architecturally compatible with Echoelmusic's typed bus. Its value lies in the interpretable middle layer (descriptors that can be logged and shown to the user), not in the diffusion model. Running a large diffusion model on-device in real time is a separate feasibility question (next section).

### Gaps
- No quantitative cross-study accuracy for HRV-only (wrist/finger PPG) emotion or arousal classification was retrieved.
- No peer-reviewed RL + live-physiology + *generative* music system was found. This is an open research gap (and a possible novelty claim, if it is one day built and evaluated).
- Camera rPPG HRV accuracy at the short windows Echoelmusic uses (~10 s) was not retrieved.

## Generative visuals/light on-device: real-time neural vs procedural, bio/audio-reactive, flash safety

### Takeaway
Real-time neural image effects on phones have been feasible since ~2016 for small feed-forward models (sub-50 ms on optimised paths). Throughput depends heavily on hardware, and current Apple Neural Engine latency figures were not found. For a bio-reactive instrument at a pinned 60 fps, procedural Metal shaders remain the robust core; any neural layer is an optional, lower-rate stylisation. WCAG 2.3.1 defines the flash constraint precisely, and it applies equally to DMX light output.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta (2016):** on-device camera style transfer with inference under 50 ms on some phones; the style models needed separate optimisation to hold real-time frame rates at high resolution. — [Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2016/11/08/android/delivering-real-time-ai-in-the-palm-of-your-hand/)
- **Software Mansion / React Native ExecuTorch:** pre-packaged style-transfer models run on-device via Core ML (Apple) or XNNPACK (CPU), and media never leaves the device. — [ExecuTorch docs](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-executorch/docs/extensions/style-transfer)
- In the same team's tests, a cloud pipeline capped out at about 20 fps, and the real-time claims of feed-forward methods held only on capable GPUs. — [Software Mansion blog](https://magic.swmansion.com/blog/client-side-ai-model-inference-for-real-time-video-processing-style-transfer-52be282968f9/)
- Efficient vision-transformer style transfer tuned by neural architecture search for phones and low-power boards (IJCAI 2023 / ACML 2022 listing). The citation metadata is inconsistent, so verify before quoting. — [IBM Research listing](https://researcher.watson.ibm.com/publications/real-time-style-transfer-with-efficient-vision-transformers)
- **WCAG 2.3.1 (Level A), "Three Flashes or Below Threshold":** content must not flash more than three times in any one-second period, unless it stays below the general and red flash thresholds.
  - A *flash* is a pair of opposing relative-luminance changes of ≥10% where the darker state is below 0.80.
  - A violation requires all three of: more than 3 flashes per second, below 50 Hz, and a combined area above 0.006 sr (about 25% of any 10° field).
  - A *red flash* is a transition involving saturated red: R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8 and a change in (R−G−B)×320 greater than 20. People are more sensitive to red flashing.
  - Testing normally needs specialised software (sufficient technique G15).
  - — [W3C Understanding 2.3.1 (WCAG 2.2)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [DAISY KB](https://kb.daisy.org/publishing/docs/wcag/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)
- DAISY states that a flashing area of ≤21,824 px² passes. This figure was not found in the W3C text, so treat it as unverified. — [DAISY KB](https://kb.daisy.org/publishing/docs/conformance/wcag/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)

### Inferences
- **Flash safety extends beyond the screen.** WCAG is written for screen content. A DMX rig is *larger* in the visual field than any phone, so the same ≤3 Hz rule and a red-flash rule should apply to Art-Net/sACN output (strobe channels, full-field dimmer swings), arguably more strictly. *Pulse Room*-style per-heartbeat blinking is within the rule up to 180 bpm, but a heartbeat-driven strobe combined with an audio-onset strobe could exceed it. Any limiter should count opposing transitions across *all* sources together, not per source.
- The red-flash definition gives a concrete, testable rule that a "max 3 Hz" frequency cap alone does not cover: a slow but saturated red full-field pulse needs its own check.
- **Architecture fit:** keep the 60 fps procedural Metal path as the performance-critical layer. A neural element (style transfer, latent-space morphing), if ever added, belongs at the ~1 Hz bio-application rate, i.e. parameter changes interpolated by the shader, not per-frame inference. This mirrors the repo's 10 Hz poll vs ~1 Hz application split, and it keeps thermal headroom.
- Interpretable mapping is also a science-first visual principle: "every visual element must reflect actual data" (the repo's own law) favours parameter-mapped procedural visuals over opaque neural imagery whose relation to the body cannot be shown.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) Core ML / Apple Neural Engine latency benchmarks for real-time generative image models on iPhone were found.
- No sourced documentation of TouchDesigner, Resolume, MadMapper or grandMA biosignal practice (OSC/sACN bridging conventions) was retrieved. Nothing is claimed about it.
- No standard was found for photosensitive-safety limits on *stage lighting/DMX* specifically (theatre/event strobe guidance would need a separate search, e.g. UK Ofcom/ITC guidance or venue strobe policies).
