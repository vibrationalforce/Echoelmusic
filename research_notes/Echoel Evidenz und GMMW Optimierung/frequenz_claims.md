# Evidenz zu "Healing Frequencies" und Brain-Entrainment-Claims (Stand 2026-10-09)

Scope note: "Resonara" (Skool) and "Michael's Music Mind" could not be found by web search (queries "Resonara Skool community frequency" and "\"Michael's Music Mind\"" returned only unrelated hits: Resonance FM, Michael Mind Project, a Music Mind podcast/book). They are treated below as typical resonance/frequency/music-brain offerings.

## Solfeggio (528 Hz) und 432-Hz-Stimmung: Herkunft und kontrollierte Studien

### Takeaway
The human evidence is a handful of very small studies from a single group or of pilot quality, with borderline results. There is no replicated, preregistered RCT showing a frequency-specific effect. Popular claims such as "528 Hz repairs DNA" or "528 Hz raises testosterone" rest on rat studies at 100 dB, or on nothing at all.

### Cited Findings
- Akimoto et al. 2018 (journal *Health*) compared music at 528 Hz with the same music at 440 Hz. After about 5 minutes, cortisol fell and oxytocin rose, and tension/anxiety mood scores fell only in the 528 Hz condition. The sample was tiny, and it measured no testosterone. — [MedCrave summary](https://medcraveonline.com/IJCAM/certain-frequency-music-has-attracted-attention-forpossible-effective-healing.html); [summary](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-528-hz-good-for-benefits-and-claims-explained/)
- The "528 Hz testosterone" claim comes from a rat study in *Genes & Genomics* (2019, epub 2018): 528 Hz at 100 dB increased testosterone in rat brain tissue. It is animal work at a very high sound level. — [PubMed 30414050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30414050/)
- Calamassi & Pomponi 2019 (*Explore*) was a double-blind crossover pilot with n=33. Heart rate was −4.79 bpm lower at 432 Hz than at 440 Hz, with p = 0.05 (borderline). The sample was small and the order was not randomised. — [summary with critique, Sandra Garrido, Univ. of Sydney](https://www.yourlifechoices.com.au/health/does-432hz-tuning-improve-your-wellbeing-a-music-psychologist-unpacks-the-evidence/)
- A 2025 RCT crossover in cancer patients (BMC Complement Med Ther) compared 432 Hz with 443 Hz. Heart rate fell in both conditions (median −3 vs −1 bpm, p=0.04). However, the authors themselves conclude the effect is "more pronounced but not significantly different" on objective cardiovascular parameters. — [PMC11755923](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11755923/)
- A music psychologist sums up the field: effects on wellbeing come "less about any single special frequency, and more about how we perceive and interpret sound." — [Garrido](https://www.yourlifechoices.com.au/health/does-432hz-tuning-improve-your-wellbeing-a-music-psychologist-unpacks-the-evidence/)

### Inferences
- 432 Hz vs 440 Hz is a pitch shift of about 32 cents. Any effect is plausibly explained by expectation, the relaxing music itself, or slightly lower brightness. A "resonance with nature/DNA" explanation has no physical basis.
- The Solfeggio origin story (Horowitz/Puleo, 1990s, an esoteric reading of a Gregorian hymn) did not turn up in a scholarly source during this search. It is therefore not cited here, and it belongs in Gaps.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for the historical origin of the Solfeggio numbers or of the "Verdi pitch / 432 Hz" myth.
- No systematic review specifically on 432 Hz / 528 Hz was found.

## Binaural Beats, Auditory Beat Stimulation, isochronic Töne

### Takeaway
On behavioural outcomes (anxiety, memory, attention, pain), the 2019 meta-analysis finds a moderate pooled effect (g=0.45). Later work shows mixed results, and the EEG entrainment hypothesis that the marketing rests on is NOT reliably supported: 5 of 14 studies agree with it, 8 contradict it. The evidence on isochronic tones is very thin. Auditory risk is low. The real seizure risk lies in visual flicker.

### Cited Findings
- Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales 2019, *Psychological Research* 83(2):357–372, pooled 22 studies with 35 effect sizes. The overall effect was medium, significant and consistent (g = 0.45). Exposure before or before-and-during a task worked better than exposure only during it. Longer exposure helped. Masking with noise made no difference. — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)
- Ingendoh, Posny & Heine 2023, PLOS ONE, is a systematic review (not a meta-analysis) of EEG entrainment. Of 14 studies, 5 were consistent with the entrainment hypothesis, 8 contradicted it and 1 was mixed. Methods were highly heterogeneous, and the authors call for standardisation. — [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)
- Basu & Banerjee 2023, *Psychological Research*, is a meta-analysis on memory and attention. It reports mixed results across the cognitive domains. Its pooled effect sizes were not retrieved. — [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/)
- Chaieb et al. 2015, *Frontiers in Psychiatry* (Bonn, epileptology), is a PRISMA review of auditory beat stimulation. It calls the approach a "promising tool" and notes contradictory findings driven by stimulation parameters. — [PMC4428073](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4428073/)
- In a 2021 review, 15 of 17 studies used binaural beats and only 2 used isochronic tones, which shows how thin the isochronic research base is. — [Revista Mexicana de Neurociencia](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/rmn/v22n6/2604-6180-rmn-22-6-238.pdf)
- Risk from sound: no peer-reviewed case of binaural beats causing a seizure was found. The cautions in circulation are "theoretical" and come from commercial or blog sources. — [Dr. Weil](https://drweil.com/?p=106708) (secondary, low quality)
- Risk from light: photosensitive seizures affect about 1 in 10,000 people. People with epilepsy have a 2–14 % risk from light or pattern stimuli, and the critical flash range is 10–25 Hz. — [Epilepsy Australia factsheet May 2026](https://www.epilepsy.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Factsheet_Photosensitive-epilepsy_May2026.pdf); [NCT04188834 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/34/NCT04188834/Prot_SAP_001.pdf)

### Inferences
- A modest behavioural effect plus unreliable EEG entrainment fits relaxation, expectation or arousal effects better than "the brain locks onto the frequency". The claim "puts your brain into theta/delta" goes beyond the evidence.
- For audiovisual offerings (and for Echoel's visuals), the relevant safety rule is the ≤3 Hz flash ceiling (WCAG), not the audio.

### Gaps
- Separate effect sizes for anxiety and pain from Garcia-Argibay were not extracted, because the full text was not read.
- No Cochrane review on binaural beats was found.

## Gestützte rhythmische/neurale Entrainment-Anwendungen

### Takeaway
Rhythmic auditory cueing (RAS) for gait in Parkinson's disease is the best-supported "music entrains the body" application. The mechanism is sensorimotor synchronisation, not a special frequency. 40 Hz gamma sensory stimulation for Alzheimer's is investigational and contested: the mouse findings are contradictory, the human data come from a company-run pilot, and a pivotal readout had not been found as of 2026-10.

### Cited Findings
- Ghai et al. 2018, *Scientific Reports*, pooled 50 studies with 1,892 participants. Auditory cueing improved gait speed and stride length. — [PMC5764963](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5764963/)
- Wang et al. 2022, *Frontiers in Neurology*, reviewed 21 RCTs (14 meta-analysed). RAS improved velocity, step length and stride length, as well as 6MWT, TUG and UPDRS-II/III. Cadence and stride width did not change significantly. — [PMC9053573](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9053573/)
- Soula et al. 2023, *Nature Neuroscience* (Buzsáki lab): 40 Hz light flicker did not entrain native gamma in the visual cortex, entorhinal cortex or hippocampus, and did not reduce plaque. The Tsai group disputes the findings. — [Alzforum](https://alzforum.org/papers/forty-hertz-light-stimulation-does-not-entrain-native-gamma-oscillations-alzheimers-disease); [bioRxiv response](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.10.30.564197.full.pdf)
- An eNeuro 2023 study found that 5 weeks of 40 Hz light did not lower Aβ in 5xFAD mice. — [eNeuro](https://www.eneuro.org/content/10/8/ENEURO.0189-23.2023)
- Cognito's OVERTURE feasibility study (Spectris, 40 Hz light plus sound) was small and its results are company-reported. Safety and adherence were good, with no ARIA. Its efficacy signals come from post-hoc analyses. — [Frontiers in Neurology 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10957179); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/cognitos-sensory-stimulation-device-slows-decline-in-alzheimers/)
- The HOPE pivotal trial (NCT05637801) is randomised, double-blind and sham-controlled, with 670 participants at 70 US sites, 12 months of treatment and endpoints of ADCS-ADL and MMSE. Enrolment finished in mid-2025, and a readout was guided for mid/August 2026. No result was found in searches on 2026-10-09. The device has FDA Breakthrough Device Designation but is not approved. — [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/cognito-therapeutics-completes-enrollment-in-landmark-alzheimer-s-trial); [Business Wire 2025-04](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250402055525/en/Cognito-Therapeutics-Announces-Spectris-Data-Demonstrating-Brain-Structural-Maintenance-and-Mechanism-of-Action-in-Alzheimers-Disease-at-ADPD-2025)

### Inferences
- What genuinely carries evidence (beat-based gait cues, a medical-device 40 Hz protocol under trial) is narrow, protocol-bound and clinical. It does not justify generic "music-brain" wellness claims.
- Anyone advertising "40 Hz against dementia" today is advertising an unproven therapy.

### Gaps
- The HOPE topline status as of 2026-10 is unconfirmed. Check the company site or registry.
- No Cochrane review on RAS in Parkinson's was found.

## Regulatorik / Werberecht für Gesundheits-Claims

### Takeaway
In Germany, the HWG (Heilmittelwerbegesetz) applies to "procedures and treatments" and prohibits attributing effects that are not scientifically established. Case law sets particularly strict standards for health claims, including on bioresonance. The FTC expects RCTs for health benefit claims. Apple 1.4.1 rejects apps whose health information is unvalidated or unsourced. The EU Health Claims Regulation (1924/2006) covers food only and is not directly relevant to audio offerings; this is an inference, see Gaps.

### Cited Findings
- § 3 HWG: advertising is misleading in particular if it attributes to procedures, treatments, objects or other means a therapeutic efficacy or effects they do not have (No. 1), or if it creates the impression that success can be expected with certainty or that there are no harmful effects (No. 2). — [gesetze-im-internet.de](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilmwerbg/__3.html)
- OLG München, 8.12.2016, 29 U 1893/16 (bioresonance therapy): health advertising must meet particularly strict requirements of accuracy and clarity. — [omsels.info](https://www.omsels.info/die-verbote-oder-was-darf-ich-nicht/2-heilmittelwerbegesetz/3-irrefuehrende-werbung/nr-1-wirksamkeitwirkungen)
- BGH I ZR 204/19 (Sinupret): animal and in-vitro data without clinical relevance do not substantiate efficacy claims. KG Berlin 5 U 9/24 (2024) found osteopathy advertising misleading for lack of scientific backing. — [omsels.info](https://www.omsels.info/die-verbote-oder-was-darf-ich-nicht/2-heilmittelwerbegesetz/3-irrefuehrende-werbung/nr-1-wirksamkeitwirkungen)
  - Relevance: this applies directly to "528 Hz" testosterone/DNA claims, which rest on rat or in-vitro data.
- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022) covers all health claims. Benefit claims generally require randomised, controlled, double-blind human studies; a second, independent RCT is recommended. Animal, in-vitro and anecdotal evidence is generally insufficient. "May/helps" qualifiers do not cure an unsupported claim, and post-hoc analyses are suspect. — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2023/01/ftc-issues-new-guidance-on-health-related-claims-to-replace-the-dietary-supplements-advertising-guide); [BBB Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/ftc-health-claims-guidance)
- Apple App Store Guideline 1.4.1: medical apps that could deliver inaccurate data or be used for diagnosis or treatment face extra scrutiny. Accuracy claims need disclosed data and methodology. Health recommendations need in-app source citations, and apps should remind users to consult a doctor. — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/703502); [ConductAtlas copy of guideline](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/healthcare-and-medical-app-requirements/); [wellness-vs-medical thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807508)

### Inferences
- Claims such as "healing frequencies", "DNA repair", "cures anxiety" or "brain synchronisation for dementia" would, by these standards, be at risk under the HWG (heilmittelwerberechtlich) and likely FTC-actionable. Neutral wording (subjective relaxation, music experience) is defensible.
- For Echoel this confirms the CLAUDE.md rule: no esoteric terminology, biofeedback as a measured modulation source rather than therapy, and claim only what is measured.

### Gaps
- EU Reg. 1924/2006 was not checked in its original text; its food-only scope is stated from background knowledge.
- The UWG §5 (general misleading advertising) and the EU MDR "intended purpose" threshold for software were not researched with sources.
- No court decision specifically on "frequency music" was found.
- The guideline wording comes from third-party copies; the developer.apple.com original was not loaded.
